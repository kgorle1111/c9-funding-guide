## 2. Application / cover note (why you, why this job) — ~180 words

When I became VP of Finance for the College Nine Senate, our funding process lived in scattered emails. So I built us a real website in Drupal 11: custom content types for funding opportunities, a webform for applications, a budget transparency dashboard, a hand-written CSS sub-theme, and photos I cropped and resized myself so every page felt finished. I also wrote the guidelines PDF and recorded a narrated demo, because a site only works if people can actually use it.

That project taught me the job behind this job: clear writing, consistent visuals, and details a visitor never notices because they were done right. Earlier, as Senator and VP of External Affairs, I helped grow event engagement 17.6% across 1,650+ students through social media and flyers, so I think about content the way an audience reads it, not the way a developer builds it.

I've already started with Drupal, and I'm genuinely eager to learn Slate and Admissions' editorial standards. UCSC convinced me to come here; helping the Admissions site do that for the next student is work I'd be proud of.

Word count: ~185.

## 3. Demo video narration outlines

### 60-second version
1. (0:00–0:08) On camera or voiceover over the site homepage: "I'm Kannishk, VP of Finance for College Nine Senate. Our funding info was scattered, so I built this site."
2. (0:08–0:18) Photopea: crop and resize a campus photo, mention keeping images fast and consistent.
3. (0:18–0:32) Drupal: open a funding page, edit a heading and body text, show it's plain and safe to update.
4. (0:32–0:40) Click Save/Publish, show the live page updating.
5. (0:40–0:50) Fill and submit the funding request webform as a student would.
6. (0:50–1:00) Budget transparency page: "Every dollar students give us, visible. That's the standard I'd bring to Admissions." End card with name + links.

### 3-minute version
1. (0:00–0:20) Framing: who I am, the problem (funding process buried in email threads), the assignment I gave myself: one clear site any student can navigate.
2. (0:20–0:50) Content plan: show the site map briefly. Explain pages were written for students first: short sentences, obvious next steps, a downloadable guidelines PDF.
3. (0:50–1:20) Photos: in Photopea, crop a real campus photo to the banner size, resize for the web, drop it into the page. Note why: consistent sizes keep the site looking professional and loading fast.
4. (1:20–2:00) Editing in Drupal 11: open a funding opportunity page, edit text, swap the image, preview. Emphasize that anyone on the team could do this after ten minutes of training, which is the point of a CMS.
5. (2:00–2:20) Publish and view live. Quick note that the demo is hosted on GitHub Pages so the committee can click around it today.
6. (2:20–2:45) Student flow: submit the funding request form end to end, show the confirmation.
7. (2:45–3:00) Budget transparency dashboard: where every funded request shows up publicly. Close: "This is the kind of clear, honest web presence I'd love to help Admissions build. Thanks for watching."

Non-technical viewer rules: never say "content type," "sub-theme," or "static build" on camera; say "page template," "the site's look," "the demo site." Show the screen doing the thing before naming it.
