# Persona Walkthrough: "Priya" vs. the C9 Funding Site

**Method note:** Qualitative simulation of the planned content (`content/*.md`, nav order per `PLAN.md`), not statistical evidence. Findings are hypotheses to validate with 2–3 real club officers. Copy is judged as written; quotes are verbatim from the content files.

## Persona

- **Priya**, 19F, sophomore. Treasurer of a 30-member UCSC cultural club — **not a College Nine org**.
- Needs **~$350** for a spring showcase; catering is mentally already in the budget.
- First-ever Senate funding request. Skims on her phone between classes. Stressed, deadline-driven.
- Arrival: Googled "UCSC club funding" or got the link from a friend's GroupMe. Lands on **Home**.
- Fears: wasting weeks on a rejection; looking clueless in front of a formal board; hidden rules.
- Fogg baseline: Motivation HIGH (event has a date), Ability UNKNOWN, will bail to a co-officer group chat at the first sign of ambiguity.

---

## Page 1: Home (`home.md`)

**Priya's monologue (0–10 seconds):**
> "'Got an event, a project, or a program that could use some money?' Okay, yes, literally me. 'The College Nine Senate funds student ideas — yours included.' ...wait. College Nine. We're not a College Nine club. Does 'yours included' mean me, or College Nine people? It doesn't say. I'll keep going and hope."

**The 10-second eligibility test: FAIL (soft).** The hero copy is warm and the four-step list is genuinely clear — "That's the whole process. No hidden steps, no paperwork maze" is the best line on the page and directly lowers anxiety. But nothing above or below it answers *"is a non-C9 org eligible, and on what terms?"* Priya's single most important question at second five gets zero words. "Yours included" is doing eligibility work it can't actually do — it reads as inclusive but confirms nothing.

**Continuing scroll:**
> "Step 3, 'You present for 3–5 minutes at a weekly Senate meeting.' Ugh. In person? To a senate? ... okay, 3–5 minutes, and it's every Thursday, that's not an interview I guess. Step 4, decision in 2–3 weeks. Hm. Showcase is in like six weeks, so submit + wait for presentation + 2–3 weeks... that's tight but doable IF I start now. 'Read the funding guidelines — a few minutes now saves you a resubmission later.' Yeah yeah. Where's the form?"

**Analyst — Home:**
- Emotional arc: relief → eligibility doubt → mild presentation dread → time-pressure math → impatience.
- **LIFT Clarity: high** for process, **Relevance: unresolved** for the outside-org visitor. **Anxiety: partially handled** — "No hidden steps, no paperwork maze" and "We're students too — we'll give you a straight answer" are both doing real de-fanging work. Quote them in any future rewrite; they're the voice the whole site should keep.
- **Timeline math:** "decision by email, typically within 2–3 weeks" on the home page is honest and lets Priya do her spring-planning math immediately — good. But it never says *plan backward from your event date*, and nothing says whether the 2–3 weeks starts at submission or at presentation (how-to-request says "of your submission"; home is ambiguous). For a spring event with venue deadlines, that ambiguity is a real risk.
- **Fogg:** Motivation high, Prompt present ("Submit a request using the form"), but Ability is throttled by the unanswered eligibility question. The most likely Priya behavior: **skip "Before you apply," tap Request Form in the nav.** Nav order (Home → How to Request → Guidelines → FAQ → Transparency → Request Form) invites exactly this — the form is one tap away, the $200 rule is two pages deep.

**Critical structural finding:** On the planned nav, Priya can travel Home → Request Form and encounter the $200 outside-org limit and the food exclusion **only if the form itself surfaces them**. PLAN.md Phase 3 has "Policies linked at the top of the form + required 'I have read the guidelines' checkbox" — a link and a checkbox are speed bumps, not information. A stressed skimmer checks the box unread; everyone does.

## Page 2: How to Request (`how-to-request.md`)

(Assume she taps this — it's next in the nav and named like an instruction manual, which suits her.)

**Priya's monologue:**
> "Step 1, check the guidelines... 'A few things — like food costs — can't be funded.' WAIT. What? No food? The showcase budget is half catering. Okay that's — ugh. At least it says it here and not after I submitted. Step 4, the presentation... 'Keep it simple. Tell us what you're planning, who it's for, what it costs, and how it connects to College Nine.' Okay, that's four sentences, I can do four sentences. That actually helps. Scrolling... 'Heads up on request limits. Organizations outside College Nine can request up to $200.' ...We're asking for $350. So we CAN'T? No wait — 'Requests above that need detailed written justification — otherwise the request is returned for revision.' So we can, with an essay. Why is this at the BOTTOM? I almost closed the tab at $200."

**Analyst — How to Request:**
- **The food exclusion reaches her here — barely, and secondhand.** The Step 1 aside ("like food costs") is the only pre-guidelines mention, and it's an example inside a "go read the other page" instruction. It DID land for Priya, but only because it's a concrete noun; a skimmer scanning bold text misses it entirely (nothing food-related is bolded on this page). Verdict: the exclusion arrives *before* she budgets in a formal sense but *after* she budgeted in her head — the damage-control moment could come one page earlier.
- **The $200 limit: discovered AFTER she's mentally committed, positioned last, titled vaguely.** "Heads up on request limits" is a Step-6-of-5 afterthought below "Get your decision." For an outside org, this is not a heads-up; it is the gating rule. Priya's read order was: full process → emotional buy-in → gate. That ordering manufactures the exact whiplash the site exists to prevent. Also, the copy leads with the ceiling ("$200, per the Constitution") and buries the escape hatch ("detailed written justification") in sentence two — a skimmer who bails at "$200" never learns $350 is possible. **LIFT Anxiety spike, self-inflicted by ordering.**
- **The presentation de-fanging works.** "Prepare a **3–5 minute visual presentation**" plus "Keep it simple. Tell us what you're planning, who it's for, what it costs, and how it connects to College Nine" converts an intimidating abstraction into a checklist. The "What makes a strong presentation" bullets ("A clear, itemized budget — where each dollar goes") give a nervous first-timer a literal slide outline. This is the strongest section on the site. **Fogg Ability: raised exactly where it needed raising.**
- One gap in the de-fanging: nothing says the audience is friendly, that first-timers are normal, or what happens if she flubs it (can she clarify by email after?). For an anxious first-time presenter, "you'll give it at a weekly Senate meeting" still reads like a jury. One sentence of warmth ("most presenters are first-timers; we ask friendly questions, not gotchas") would finish the job the "Keep it simple" line started.
- **Timeline:** "Decisions typically arrive within **2–3 weeks** of your submission" — the "of your submission" anchor is correct and only appears here. For a spring showcase ~6 weeks out, this works *if she starts now*, but no page ever says "submit at least N weeks before your event." "Submit as early as you can — especially if your event has a fixed date" gestures at it without giving her a number to act on. Stressed people need the number.

## Page 3: Funding Guidelines (`funding-guidelines.md`)

**Priya's monologue:**
> "'Read this before you submit. It's short' — okay, it actually is short, thank you. '**Food costs are not funded.** No exceptions — plan your budget without them.' Yeah, I know now. Blunt but at least it's not fine print. Costumes 'typically not funded' — oh no, the dance sets have costumes. 'Typically'? So maybe? Who decides? ... '$200' again, and 'detailed written justification... include it the first time.' Fine. What counts as detailed? A paragraph? A doc? 'Itemize your budget. Lump sums raise questions; line items answer them.' Okay, that's actually a useful sentence. I'm making a spreadsheet."

**Analyst — Guidelines:**
- This page is well-ordered for HER (exclusions first, limit second, tips last) — the problem is she reaches it third, or never. As the destination of the form's "I have read" checkbox it's fine; as the safety net it's too far downstream.
- "**Food costs are not funded.** No exceptions — plan your budget without them" — model copy: bolded, absolute, and it tells her what to DO, not just what's banned. Same for "Lump sums raise questions; line items answer them." Keep both verbatim.
- Two anxiety leaks:
  1. "Costume expenses are **typically** not funded" / travel "**typically** not considered" — for a cultural club whose showcase IS costumes, "typically" is a coin-flip with her budget. No criteria, no example of an exception, no "email us to check" adjacency to that line specifically. (The page-end "Not sure whether your expense qualifies? Ask before you submit" partially covers it — good instinct, wrong distance from the trigger.)
  2. "detailed written justification" is required twice on the site and defined zero times. For the persona whose request is $350 — i.e., the person this clause governs — the site never says what a passing justification looks like. That's the single highest-stakes undefined term on the site.

## Page 4: FAQ (`faq.md`)

**Priya's monologue:**
> "'Can we get food funded? No. ... full stop.' Harsh but honest. 'Can organizations outside College Nine apply? Yes.' — okay THERE it is, the actual word 'Yes.' Why isn't that sentence on the home page? 'Do we really have to present in person? Yes... It's short, and it's your best chance to make the case for your idea.' That reframe is kind of nice, actually. 'Can we reapply after a denial? Yes. A denial isn't the end.' Good to know. Okay. I think I can do this: no catering, $350 with a justification doc, costumes are a dice roll I need to email about."

**Analyst — FAQ:**
- The FAQ is where the site's best direct answers live — "Can organizations outside College Nine apply? **Yes.**" is the exact sentence the home page is missing, and "A denial isn't the end" is the exact reassurance the anxious first-timer needs. Both are stranded four pages deep.
- Question order mirrors the Senate's concerns (food first), not the visitor's (eligibility first). Priya's #1 question is answer #3.
- "Do we really have to present in person?" — the question itself is smart (it names the fear), and "It's short, and it's your best chance to make the case for your idea" reframes the requirement as her asset. Effective de-fanging.

## Page 5: Transparency (`transparency-intro.md`)

**Priya's monologue:**
> "Budget, spent, remaining... oh, this is actually useful — if remaining funds are low in spring, is $350 even realistic? 'you can time your request better and set a realistic budget' — huh, they literally tell me to use it that way. Okay, checking this before I write the ask."

**Analyst:** Sleeper hit for this persona. "When you can see what's been funded and what's left, you can time your request better and set a realistic budget" turns a compliance page into a planning tool, and seeing other orgs' award amounts (Cialdini: social proof) is the only place a first-timer can calibrate whether $350 is normal. Nothing on How to Request points here as a *planning step* — it should.

## The Four Test Questions — Verdicts

| Question | Verdict |

| Non-C9 eligibility clear within 10s? | **No.** Home never addresses it; the explicit "Yes" is in FAQ answer #3, four taps deep. |
| $200 limit discovered before writing the $350 request? | **Only if she reads How to Request to the bottom.** The nav lets her go Home → Form; on the current form plan (link + checkbox) she'd discover it at revision-return time — 1–2 weeks lost. Even on the reading path, the limit lands as a bottom-of-page ambush. |
| Food exclusion before budgeting catering? | **Marginal.** One unbolded aside on How to Request; guaranteed only on Guidelines/FAQ. She's already mentally budgeted catering before any page tells her. |

## Ranked Fixes

### 1. Put a "Who can apply" answer on Home, above or inside "How it works" — `home.md`
Two sentences: *"Any UCSC student org can apply — you don't have to be a College Nine organization. Orgs outside College Nine can request up to $200 (more with written justification — see the guidelines)."* Lifts the FAQ's stranded "Yes" and the gating number to second five of the visit. **Impact: highest.** Fixes the failed 10-second test AND puts the $200 rule ahead of every $350 mental budget. (LIFT: Relevance + Anxiety; Fogg: unblocks Ability at the moment Motivation peaks.)

### 2. Move the $200 limit from the bottom of How to Request into Step 1/2, lead with the escape hatch — `how-to-request.md`
Kill the "Heads up on request limits" afterthought placement. In Step 2 (Submit the form): *"Outside-C9 org asking for more than $200? That's allowed — attach detailed written justification with the form, or the request comes back for revision."* Leading with "That's allowed" prevents the bail-at-$200 misread the current sentence order invites. **Impact: high** — converts the walkthrough's worst whiplash moment into a prep instruction.

### 3. Surface the deal-breakers ON the request form, not behind a checkbox — Webform (PLAN.md Phase 3)
Above the amount field, a short static callout: no food costs; $200 outside-org limit; over-$200 requires the justification field (better: make the justification textarea appear conditionally when affiliate=No and amount>$200 — Webform conditional logic does this natively). **Impact: high** — this is the only fix that protects the Home → Form skimmer path, which is the most likely path. A checkbox is not information delivery.

### 4. Define "detailed written justification" once — `funding-guidelines.md` (link from how-to-request + FAQ)
Three bullets: what to cover (why the amount, itemized breakdown, C9 benefit), rough length ("half a page is plenty"), maybe one example line. The clause governs exactly the visitors most at risk (over-$200 outside orgs) and is currently undefined everywhere. **Impact: medium-high.** (LIFT: Clarity for the highest-stakes segment.)

### 5. Bold the food exclusion in How to Request Step 1 — `how-to-request.md`
Change the aside to: *"A few things — **food costs are never funded** — can't be covered, so check before you budget."* One bolding + "before you budget." **Impact: medium**, one-minute edit. Catches the scanner the current unbolded aside misses.

### 6. Add a backward-planning line wherever the timeline appears — `home.md` + `how-to-request.md`
*"Planning around a date? Submit at least 4 weeks before your event."* (Or whatever the true number is: 1–2 days scheduling + presentation + 2–3 weeks decision ≈ 4.) Also fix Home's step 4 to say "within 2–3 weeks **of your submission**" so both pages anchor the clock the same way. **Impact: medium** — spring-event planners get a number instead of arithmetic homework.

### 7. Give "typically" some edges on costumes/travel — `funding-guidelines.md`
Either state the criterion or put the escape valve on the same line: *"Costume expenses are typically not funded — if costumes are core to your event, email us before you submit and we'll tell you straight."* Reuses the site's own voice ("straight answer"). **Impact: medium** for cultural/performance orgs specifically — Priya's costume question is currently a coin-flip she has to gamble a request on.

### 8. Reorder FAQ: eligibility first — `faq.md`
Move "Can organizations outside College Nine apply?" to question #1, food to #2. Visitor's priority order, not Senate's. **Impact: low-medium**, zero-cost reorder.

### 9. Add one warmth sentence to Step 4 — `how-to-request.md`
After "Keep it simple...": *"Most presenters are doing this for the first time — Senate questions are friendly, not a grilling, and if you forget something you can follow up by email."* **Impact: low-medium**, finishes the presentation de-fanging for anxious first-timers.

### 10. Cross-link Transparency as a planning step — `how-to-request.md` Step 1 or 2
*"Check the [transparency page] to see what's left in the budget and what similar requests were awarded — it'll help you size your ask."* **Impact: low**, but it activates the site's only social-proof asset at the moment the visitor is setting a number.
